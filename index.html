Plaque assays, shown with published data

Published data, not from my lab

A plaque assay counts infectious virus. You dilute a sample, infect a layer of cells, and each infectious particle starts a spot of infection that you can see and count. Correct that count for the dilution and the volume and you get a titer in plaque-forming units per milliliter (PFU/mL). Both figures below use numbers from the papers cited under them. I did not generate any of this data.

From counted spots to a titer

42.7 foci
Dilution counted

Inoculum

Worked example from Sloutskin and Goldstein [1] for fluorescent-focus assays with alphaherpesviruses, which applies the calculation to HSV-1 and pseudorabies virus: three replicate wells at a 10⁻⁴ dilution gave 40, 43 and 45 fluorescent foci with 100 µL inoculated. Titer is the mean count multiplied by the dilution factor, then by 10 to convert 100 µL to 1 mL. The wells are redrawn here from those counts. They are not the authors' images, and the positions of the spots are random.

What an inhibitor result looks like

Infectious HSV virus in the medium of trigeminal ganglion explant cultures from latently infected mice, measured by plaque assay on Vero cells. Berber and Rouse [2] report 5.2 × 10⁵ PFU/mL in control medium, similar levels with 0.5 mM 2-deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG), and about 2 log less virus (3.7 × 10³ PFU/mL) with 5 mM metformin (their Figure 8). They found no direct antiviral effect of either drug on the virus in vitro at these concentrations, so the result describes the explant reactivation system and not a drug acting on virus particles. Values are redrawn from the text of the paper.

Sources
